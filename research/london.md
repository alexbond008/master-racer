# South London reference for Master Racer (Brixton / Peckham / Streatham)

Purpose: visually concrete, sourced reference for procedural districts. Dusk, UK left-hand traffic, NFS Most Wanted (2004) vibe.
Conventions: **[V]** = verified in a fetched source (URL given). **[U]** = UNVERIFIED / from general knowledge or my own estimate. Sources are abbreviated as S-numbers, listed at the end. All text is summarised, not copied.

**Era note (important for a 2004 feel).** Several famous "modern" things did not exist in 2004: Peckham Levels (2017), Pop Brixton (2015), the "Electric Avenue" neon-style sign (2016), Streatham Ice and Leisure Centre + Tesco Hub (opened Nov 2013, S11). In 2004 the old 1931 Streatham Ice Arena still stood (closed 2011, S10, S11) and the Locarno/Caesars ballroom closed in 2004 (S10). Peckham Library (2000) existed. The concrete car park existed (1980s, see Peckham). Pick the era you want and drop or keep props accordingly.

The game layout (Brixton NW, Peckham NE, Streatham S) matches reality well in relative terms: Peckham really is the east one, Streatham the south one. Real distances are far larger than a game map can hold, so compress (suggest 1 game km ~ 4-5 real km) and keep the *order*: Brixton -> (A23 climbing south) -> Streatham; Brixton -> (east across Camberwell, not in the game) -> Peckham.

Shared backbone: the **A23** (London to Brighton road) runs N-S through Brixton and on to Streatham (S1, S12). Brixton Road becomes Brixton Hill, then Streatham Hill, then Streatham High Road. Use this as the long N-S "hero" road linking Brixton and Streatham.

## 1. BRIXTON (north-west district)

### 1.1 Geography
- In Lambeth, 4.3 km SSE of central London. Neighbours: Stockwell, Clapham, Streatham, Camberwell, Tulse Hill, Balham, Herne Hill (S1).
- Land rises from the former marsh in the north toward the Streatham/Norwood hills. Brixton Hill climbs south of the centre. Brockwell Park lies to the south-east (S1).
- Roads (S1, S4):
  - **Brixton Road**: runs from the Oval (Kennington) down to Brixton; part of the A23. Forks at St Matthew's church into **Effra Road** and **Brixton Hill** near Acre Lane and Coldharbour Lane. Mostly straight, roughly N-S (NNW->SSE) [direction U]. Formerly near-continuous Regency terraces (S4).
  - **Coldharbour Lane**: runs east-west from the Brixton Oval/Ritzy end toward Camberwell/Loughborough Junction [direction U; named in S1, S4].
  - **Acre Lane**: runs west from the Town Hall corner toward Clapham [U; named in S4, S8].
  - **Stockwell Road**: where Brixton Academy sits, address 211 Stockwell Road (S5). Runs north-west from the centre [U].
  - **Electric Avenue / Brixton Station Road / Atlantic Road**: market streets beside the railway (S6, S7).
- Rail: two viaducts. The Chatham Main Line station (Brixton, trains to Victoria/Orpington) sits **high above ground on a railway bridge**, entered from Atlantic Road via staircases, ~100 m north of the Underground station; two platforms (S9). Station Road has a **long row of railway arches on its southern side** used by traders, many facades rebuilt to a standard design about 10 years ago, brick and steel shutters now covered in graffiti and tags (S7). Brixton tube (Victoria line) is the terminus. [Viaduct runs roughly east-west across the A23: U.]

### 1.3 Street-level character
- Victorian and Regency terraces and villas along main roads, many converted to flats; post-war estates (Stockwell Park, Angell Town, Cowley, Loughborough, Tulse Hill, Somerleyton) mixing 1940s low blocks with 1960s-70s towers, some with high-level walkways (S1).
- Commercial core: Brixton Road, market streets, arches (S1).
- Market: stalls in the carriageway on Electric Ave / Station Road, traditionally clothes and bric-a-brac in Station Road, shrinking now (S7).
- Graffiti and murals: murals on the Underground/rail station exterior (S9), tags on arches (S7).
- Station platforms hold three bronze figures (Platforms Piece, 1986, listed 2016) (S9).
- Colours [U]: London stock brick (yellow-grey), red brick, painted shopfront fascias in bright Caribbean greens/yellows/reds, black steel shutters.

### 1.4 Procedural recipe (Brixton)
1. **Brick viaduct strip**: a continuous brick arch run (arch ~4 m wide, ~3.5-4 m high) with roller-shutter doors, plus a train crossing overhead on a deck 7-8 m above the road. Tag the brick with graffiti decals.
2. **Market street block**: 6-8 m wide street lined with 3-4 storey shop parades; 1-1.5 m wide stalls with striped canopies in the road/pavement; hanging banners and string lights across the street.
3. **One copper-domed cinema-style hero building** (Academy): cream/green panelled 2-storey front, big copper half-dome over the entrance, bright marquee lights.
4. **Clock tower landmark** (Town Hall): 41 m stone tower with lit clock faces at the A23 junction; use as the district's skyline anchor.
5. **Windrush Square-style plaza**: paved open rectangle, a church with columned portico, library, trees.
6. **Estate blocks** (Barrier Block): 6-8 storey brick horseshoe/slab with deck walkways, set back from Coldharbour Lane.
7. **A23 hill**: let the main road climb gently southward out of the district toward Streatham (Brixton Hill).
8. **Dusk lighting**: orange sodium-type street light pools, lit shop signs, cinema marquee, train windows glowing.

## 2. PECKHAM (north-east district)

### 2.1 Geography
- Southwark, ~5.6 km SE of Charing Cross. Neighbours: Bermondsey, New Cross, Camberwell, East Dulwich, Nunhead, Brockley, Walworth (S2).
- Roads (S2): **Rye Lane** is the main N-S shopping street (formerly South Street, a country lane leading to Peckham Rye, S15). Also Peckham High Street, Queens Road, Blackpool Road (bus garage), Bellenden Road (indie shops), Peckham Hill Street, Peckham Road. Rye Lane is a two-lane road, from Peckham Library/Peckham Square in the north southward, lined with low and mid-rise buildings (S15). Mostly straight, running roughly N-S [direction U].
- Rail (S2, S16): **Peckham Rye station** (1865, Charles Henry Driver, Grade II) has four platforms on two viaducts: platforms 1-2 on the south viaduct, 3-4 on the north, with a single ground-level ticket hall (S16). Entrance off Rye Lane via a 1930s forecourt arcade that is being demolished from March 2026 (S16), so the station currently has works hoardings. A viaduct runs behind the bus garage east of the station (S2). Queens Road Peckham and Nunhead stations are nearby (S2). The former Crystal Palace line closed 1954, now Brenchley Gardens (S2). Railway arches near the station are being opened up for shops (S15). A 1925 view of Rye Lane shows the railway just above delivery vans, i.e. the line crosses above the street near the station (S15).

### 2.3 Street-level character
- London stock brick, red brick and render; narrow terraced shop fronts with flats above; bold hand-painted or backlit fascia signs; Afro-Caribbean/African/South-Asian shops, hair and beauty salons, chicken shops, fruit and veg stalls, money transfer shops (S15, S19) [salon/chicken-shop detail from tourist guides]. "Cacophony of sounds and colours" (S15).
- Art Deco typography survives on the Holdron's entrance (S19). Graffiti on bridges and shutters, stickers, mix of old hand-painted and plastic shop signs (S19 Brixton Buzz photo essay).
- 1960s estate blocks and modern regeneration housing (S2).

### 2.4 Procedural recipe (Peckham)
1. **Rye Lane parade**: continuous 3-storey brick shop terrace, plots ~5-6 m wide, a different bright fascia colour every plot, hanging projecting signs, security shutters pulled half-down at dusk, upper windows with warm interior light.
2. **Pavement stalls**: 1.5-2 m deep fruit and veg stalls and rail-racks on the pavement, crates, striped awnings, tarpaulins.
3. **Railway viaduct crossing**: brick viaduct with shuttered arches crossing the street, and a train silhouette on it; low-ish arch clearance sign (see section 5).
4. **Library hero building**: inverted-L, copper-green cladding, thin diagonal steel legs, lit coloured glass panel.
5. **Concrete car park**: ~10-storey open-sided brutalist grey slab, rooftop strip lighting, ramps visible.
6. **Clock tower on the corner**: ~25-30 m Victorian-Venetian corner block with a lit clock face, as the northern gateway.
7. **Brick warehouse** (Bussey): 4-5 storey yellow-brown brick block with arched windows, rooftop festoon lights.
8. **Estate towers**: a cluster of 10-14 storey 1960s slab blocks to the north edge.

## 3. STREATHAM (southern district)

### 3.1 Geography
- Lambeth (some west parts in Wandsworth), ~8 km south of Charing Cross (S3). Neighbours: Balham, Brixton, Tooting Bec, Tulse Hill, Norbury, Thornton Heath, West Norwood (S3).
- **Streatham High Road**: ~2.9 km (1.8 mi) long, runs **north to south**, from Streatham Hill station to Norbury where it becomes London Road (A23) (S12). After ~0.6 mi a large junction brings in Tooting Bec Gardens (A214) and Mitcham Lane (A216); beyond it the road is relatively **straight** following the Roman London-Brighton line (S12). A steep dip at a crossroads known as "The Dip" (S3). Streatham Hill is the northern continuation (A23) toward Brixton Hill.
- Streatham Common lies on the southern edge, east of the High Road (S3, S12).
- Rail (S3, S22): three stations. **Streatham Hill** station: wooden station building on the High Road at Leigham Court Rd, platforms in a **cutting under a road bridge**, small entrance because the bridge structure could not support a larger one (S22). **Streatham** station (1868): main entrance on the High Road, two platforms below street level, Thameslink and Southern trains (S22b). **Streatham Common** station: ~0.8 km from the common, near Greyhound Lane bridge (S22). [Which are on embankment vs cutting: Streatham Hill in cutting is verified.]

### 3.3 Street-level character
- Late-19th-century red-brick parade architecture on the High Road, early-to-mid 20th century on Streatham Hill (S25). Red-brick Victorian and Edwardian dominate (S3).
- Large 1930s mansion blocks, cinemas, theatre, ballroom: earlier nickname "the West End of South London" (S3).
- The 2002 "worst street" poll: heavy traffic, poor pavements, tired shopfronts (S12).
- Trees and open Common to the south-east (S23).
- [U] chain-store fascias (banks, bookies, fried-chicken, discount), bus stops with shelters, lots of traffic lights.

### 3.4 Procedural recipe (Streatham)
1. **Long straight A23 High Road** ~12-14 m wide with 4 lanes + bus lane, central reservation or dashed lines, constant traffic lights.
2. **Four-storey red-brick parades**: Dutch gables, chimney stacks, banded brick, sash windows with warm window light, chain-store fascias, 6-7 m plots.
3. **1930s mansion blocks**: cream/buff brick, 5-6 storeys, curved corners and horizontal bands, set on corners.
4. **Cinema/theatre pair**: one lavish Sprague-style stone theatre block + one Art Deco cinema with a vertical blade sign and neon.
5. **Curved-roof ice arena**: long barrel-vault shed with a big sign (2004 look), large car park in front.
6. **Railway cutting + road bridge** at the north end (Streatham Hill station): High Road passes over the cutting on a short bridge; timber station kiosk.
7. **Common**: sloped grass, tree lines, woodland patch with a dark treeline backdrop, paddling-pool and cedar details.
8. **Twin church spires** around a small green; use for a skyline cue.

### 5.3 Whole-map placement rules (suggested)
- Every junction: 2 traffic lights each side + pedestrian heads, yellow box markings, double yellow lines on side streets.
- Every ~150-250 m on high streets: zebra crossing + beacons.
- Bus stops every ~300 m on main roads, with red bus and shelters; bus lane red tarmac on kerb side for A23 stretches.
- Railway bridges: low-clearance sign + warning chevron paint, narrower carriageway.
- Residential side streets: speed humps every 60-80 m, keep-left bollard islands at chicanes, 5 m lamp posts with orange glow, pillar box at corners, phone box in shopping areas, TfL tube roundel at stations.
- Taxis ~10 % of traffic; buses ~5-8 %; plenty of white vans and small hatchbacks [U].
